Ombak sound prototype · inspired by Balinese gamelan · for the M-VAVE FM-1

Press Space to start the cycle. Lead the core melody with Q…I A…K (the FM-1's 16 white keys); the ensemble heads for each note you play and lands on it at the next beat. 1…0 - are the 11 black keys: the gongs by hand, the cues, the elaborating parts in and out. The FM-1's buttons and encoders are under its screen (HOME, below), and on the keyboard as in Penumbra's emulator. Headphones help: the gongs are low.

Space

Penumbra's reverb: eight moving lines with a shimmer in the loop. An octave fits any tuning; a fifth or a twelfth is not in slendro or pelog, so it colours the set.

Tides

One slow swell under everything, as in Penumbra: the space breathes, the mallets brighten, the ensemble swells, the ombak quickens and slows.

What you are hearing

Every instrument is a pair: pengumbang (lower) and pengisep (higher), tuned the Ombak knob's number of beats per second apart, so each note shimmers. The bars and kettles are a few FM operators at inharmonic ratios; the gongs add a slow hum that swells after the stroke. The core part (pokok) sounds on the beat; the interlocking pair (kotekan: polos on the beat, sangsih between) and the reyong leave each core note and turn toward the next one before it arrives. Your keys play the gendèr at once and set the next core note. With no new note, the parts circle the one they have.

Tunings and sources

    Balinese pelog modes (selisir, tembung, sunaren, baro) and the tone names in each follow the table in Wikipedia's Pelog article; baro has several interpretations, this is one common one. Which measured tone is named ding in the slendro sets is this prototype's assumption (the lowest listed tone), still to be checked.

    Respect

    Ombak is an instrument inspired by gamelan, not a gamelan. Gamelan is living music, made by communities in Bali, Java and around the world; the names, tunings and forms here are learned from them, and simplified. The colotomic patterns are sketches in the spirit of Balinese forms, not specific pieces. Hear the real thing, and learn from its players.
